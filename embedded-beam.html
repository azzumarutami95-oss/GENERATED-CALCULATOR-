Embedded head plate connection

The steel member is welded to a solid head plate embedded in concrete. Tension is transferred by bearing beneath the plate.

Preliminary checks for axial tension and single-axis bending. Tension breakout is evaluated using an equivalent CCD model. Pullout, side-face blowout, shear transfer, required ties, weld groups and stability are calculated in Advanced Checks. Each model states its scope.

01 / Steel section

Input

IWF dimensions exclude root fillets. Lₛ is measured from the plate underside to the member end. CHS / RHS are assumed unfilled; no concrete infill is credited.

02 / Head plate & concrete

Input

hₑꜰ is measured from the plate underside to the bottom concrete face. Top cover = D꜀ − hₑꜰ − tₚ. Plate and member are centered in the block. Minimum clearance is a geometry check; it does not establish breakout strength.

03 / Concrete, steel & weld materials

Input

Es is recorded as a material property; deformation is not calculated. Material grades must match the project specification.

04 / Weld & strength factors

Input

The weld connects the member end to the plate. Lw is the total installed effective length. The basic envelope check assumes uniform force distribution. The elastic weld-group check in Advanced Checks includes bending and the stated weld layout.

05 / Actions at the plate plane

Input

Enter factored action magnitudes already transferred to the plate plane. VᵤLₛ is not automatically added to Mᵤ. Positive Nᵤ pulls the member downward. IWF shear is parallel to the web; hollow-section shear acts in the depth h direction. Axial compression, torsion, load reversal and biaxial bending are not modeled.

05a / Concrete tension breakout

CCD analogy

Equivalent cast-in anchor-point CCD model; not a qualification of a large head plate. k꜀ = 10 (N, mm, MPa); normal-weight concrete λₐ = 1; ψc,N = 1.00 cracked / 1.25 uncracked. No anchor reinforcement is credited; φcb ≤ 0.70. The center and four plate corners are alternative locations of one resultant. The minimum capacity governs; capacities are not added. Implementation limits: 17 ≤ f′c ≤ 70 MPa and hₑꜰ ≥ 40 mm. For h′ₑꜰ of 280–635 mm, the deep-embedment branch is 3.9√f′c·h′ₑꜰ⁵ᐟ³; Physical hₑꜰ >635 mm is calculated with the basic expression and flagged REVIEW (extrapolation). Effective-depth correction h′ₑꜰ is applied when at least three edges are close.

05b / Ties & stability

Design assumptions

Lb, Cb and U are designer inputs, not automatically verified values. Nu is tension only; Euler compression buckling is not checked. For doubly symmetric rolled IWF sections, LTB and tension–bending interaction require a compact web. Sections outside the implemented methods are flagged REVIEW. Partial welds are assumed proportionally distributed over the full contour; a total length with an arbitrary layout is not equivalent. Splitting tie demand uses a symmetric two-strut model, T = Fₑ/(2 tan θ), evaluated separately in each direction. This is not an unreinforced-concrete cracking-strength model. n,t=0 indicates that reinforcement has not been specified. Tie capacity is credited only when development and its reference are declared.

08b / Ties, weld groups & stability

Models & applicability limits
The IWF plate load path still requires verification. Splitting is addressed by an assumed strut-and-tie design, not a plain-concrete cracking-strength equation. Side-face blowout and pullout use cast-in headed-anchor analogies. Shear transfer is checked for member bearing and pryout; shear edge breakout and complete concrete interaction remain unqualified. An OK status applies only to the stated model.

09 / Steel section properties

PROPERTYRESULT

Steel only; composite action is not credited. Normal and shear stresses are checked separately. In-scope IWF local buckling, LTB and interaction are evaluated in Advanced Checks. Holes, connection fracture and out-of-scope sections are not qualified. Shear stress is averaged over the assumed effective area.

11 / Assumptions & further checks

  1. Concrete anchorage: equivalent CCD tension breakout is evaluated in Section 08a. Pullout, side-face blowout, required splitting ties and member bearing are calculated in Advanced Checks. Applicability to the IWF plate, block thickness and the complete load path to anchor reinforcement still require verification.
  2. Plate bearing: q = Fₑ/A꜀ assumes uniform pressure beneath the plate. The limit is φc·0.85f′c, without an area-enhancement factor. Actual pressure distribution under moment, cracking and shear is not verified.
  3. Plate flexure: the elastic cantilever-strip model uses m = qa²/2 and m limit = φs fy,p tₚ²/6 per unit width. The lever arm a envelopes corner-to-member and, for IWF, plate-edge-to-web distances. This is a geometric approximation, not a two-way plate solution. Continuous support along the welded member contour is assumed. Yield lines, punching, prying and local yielding are not evaluated.
  4. Welds: the basic envelope uses Fw = √(Fₑ² + Vᵤ²) and Rw = φw·0.6FEXX·0.707sLw. Advanced Checks evaluates elastic weld-group stresses using the declared contour coverage. Verify the actual layout, orientation, effective length, weld-size limits and base-metal strength.
  5. Steel member: σ = N/A + M/S and τ = V/Aᵥ are elastic screening checks against φs fy and φs·0.6fy. Advanced Checks evaluates in-scope IWF local buckling, lateral-torsional buckling and tension–bending interaction. Slenderness values alone do not establish section adequacy.
Equivalent breakout is evaluated; overall concrete anchorage safety is not established. Ratios below 1 do not by themselves establish connection adequacy. Verify the design against the applicable project standards, including SNI 2847 anchorage / strut-and-tie provisions and SNI 1729 steel and weld provisions where relevant. The designer must establish an appropriate model for this head plate.
